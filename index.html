Severs the base so a light drops in from below: one continuous cut right around the can, flush with the bottom edge — directly under the LED wire notch, which then reads as an open U in the finished rim. Shown as the red line at the foot of the preview.
In the SVG it is written last, in its own colour, so it becomes its own layer and is cut after every hole — cut it first and the base drops away mid-job. In xTool Creative Space pick By layer and check the red layer sits at the bottom of the list; give it the passes a full through-cut needs.

Advanced

Portraits finds the face, frames the can on it and uses a much finer dot pitch — that combination is what makes a face readable from across a room. Head size is the strongest control here. Face detail sharpens the eye/nose/mouth scale specifically; past about 1.2 it starts to look harsh.

Fewer tone steps read better from across a room — the can only has about 160×120 dots to work with.

Local contrast darkens the background around a bright subject. That halo usually helps it stand out. Suppress it if bright areas are eating the detail around them.

Shadow floor keeps big dark areas from going completely blank. Edge boost traces outlines, which survive the dot grid better than fine shading does.

Same setting as Where it goes, plus Mirror — which mirrors one half into the other so the wrap matches exactly at any diameter.

“All the same” gives a flat texture: tone then comes only from how many holes there are, not how big they are.

Jitter shifts every hole off the lattice by that fraction of the pitch. Every Quality tuple uses 0.05, which keeps the rows readable; much above 0.10 the holes look scattered and the web between them shrinks twice as fast. Below 0.05 the screen starts laying dots into visible chains.
